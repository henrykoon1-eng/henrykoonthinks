<p>I write to you happily from the humble public library of Lone Pine, California.&nbsp; Two days ago I finished the &#8220;Desert&#8221; section of the Pacific Crest Trail when I passed mile 702 and entered &#8220;Kennedy Meadows&#8221;.&nbsp; Since then I have been overwhelmed by the abundant life, color, and WATER of the Sierra Nevada.&nbsp; I don&#8217;t know how to express without seeming hyperbolic the overflowing energy and happiness that this change has brought to me.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>The best I can communicate is to say this&#8211;as you picture me these days, imagine me smiling ear to ear with my eyes wide open.&nbsp; I am sticking my head in every stream, touching every sequoia tree, yelling at every marmot, cackling at every gust of wind, and stopping for every sunset. &nbsp; I am thoroughly smitten once more by my old love, true mountains.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>But, much of my ecstasy in the Sierras comes from the relief they have offered to the final stretch of the desert which was absolutely brutal.&nbsp; Somehow, we had it in our heads that the last 150 miles of desert were less desertish as they approached the mountains&#8211;this is far from the truth.&nbsp; Those 150 miles were very hard.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>[[In these descriptions I got a little more descriptive than I would have liked so feel free to skip, skim, or even just browse the pictures.]]</p><p>Day 28 [566-576] As you may remember from the last chapter, I had been invited to stay with some people at their Airbnb.&nbsp; Anyways, I woke up early to the violent radiance of the rising sun cooking me as I lay on the turf of the backyard.&nbsp; Nothing makes you so aware of your lack of sleep, the coors banquets you drank, and your miles on trail, as such a wakeup by the desert sun. {I don&#8217;t always do a good job of resting on my rest days} Then I cooked myself a bunch of eggs and my leftover bratwursts(enough of Emriique&#8217;s restaurant quality French vegan)!!!&nbsp;</p><p>I spent the next couple hours reading, journaling, and writing letters.&nbsp; It was a very good day.&nbsp; Then in the evening I ubered to the trail and began the steep ascent back up, 17 miles to water.&nbsp; That night as I hiked there was my favorite sunset yet on trail and I listened to a Jason Isbell album and felt very cinematic-and don&#8217;t you dare tell me I
