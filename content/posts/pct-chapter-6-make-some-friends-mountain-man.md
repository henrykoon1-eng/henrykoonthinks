<p>I just finished the hottest part of the desert and will begin my gradual ascent into some serious mountains, some big trees and WATER lots of water.&nbsp; I have new blisters, new pains, and new problems&#8212;yet, despite the trouble and the pain I think I am doing well.&nbsp; I think my mind is very healthy most of all.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>It is said that &#8220;a man who lives alone is either a beast or a god&#8221;.&nbsp; I would further this by saying the man who living alone becomes a beast begins to think he is a god.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>&#8212;The trail has humbled me enough to ensure I do not think myself a god and to avoid becoming a beast I knew I needed some friends.</p><p>Yes, I am writing this from an AirBnb that I am sharing with 11 other hikers as I enjoy a coors banquet.&nbsp; Last night we all cooked a big dinner together, drank together, and sang together.&nbsp; After a restful night of food and friendship with these people I will leave them to continue ahead and past them on my own pat as they stay for a full rest day.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>For reference this group has</p><p>-2 Australian couples</p><p>-a French chef</p><p>-a critical race theory scholar/ guitar player</p><p>-a ski bum</p><p>-a very familiar seeming triple crowner from Wilmington with his online girlfriend</p><p>-an overwhelmed Omish 18 year old</p><p>-a nuclear physicist/former college trackathlete</p><p>A group which is very strange for reality and very common for the pct</p><div><hr></div><p>Day 23 [454.7-478.3]-<em> Woke up in a hammock at the weird
