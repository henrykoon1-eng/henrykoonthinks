<p>I have been thinking a lot about prayer as I have been in the woods, and as I don&#8217;t have the internet or  books or big Bibles most of the time, I am limited to the prayers I have memorized.&nbsp; I am working on a larger essay about the act of prayer generally but I am writing this short essay as a preview and introduction.&nbsp; This essay will be unpacking the Lord&#8217;s Prayer.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>The Lord&#8217;s Prayer appears in both the Gospel of Mathew and the Gospel of Luke and in both cases it is given to us by Jesus.&nbsp; In Luke 11:2-4, Jesus gives the prayer when his disciples ask him &#8220;how do we pray?&#8221;&nbsp; </p><p>In Matthew 6:9-13, Jesus gives the prayer as part of his sermon on the Mount when he is as he rebukes long, public, and extravagant prayer.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>Jesus says not to pray extravagantly firstly for the reality that those who exalt themselves by the skill of their prayer make their blessing only Earthly: to enhance reputation.&nbsp; </p><p>The less obvious reason Jesus rebukes extravagant, personal, emotional prayer is that &#8220;your father knows what you need before you ask him.&#8221;&nbsp; This is the point that I think frames the structure of the prayer&#8212;that prayer is not about asking God for things but about coming into God&#8217;s will. It is about making ourselves want what God in his infinite wisdom gives us.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>This prayer is so packed with wisdom and power and  is found directly in the Bible. To me it is surprising that it is not the source of  annual sermons in every church ranging from this most to the least traditional.&nbsp; And yet, it is said blandly and often ignored by many.&nbsp; But it has been a fascination of mine in the woods!</p><p>&#8212;So without further delay, let&#8217;s dive in line by line and just scrape the surface of the infinite wisdom of Christ.</p><p><em><strong>Our Father, which art in heaven,</strong></em></p><p>God is our father, we are his sons and daughters, we are bearers of his image.&nbsp; At the same time of our filial connection to God, he is also the transcendent divinity of the heavens beyond us. </p><p><em><strong>Hallowed be thy Name,</strong></em></p><p>The Lord&#8217;s name is holy (obviously). But also as we pray this we vow to make it holy ourselves.&nbsp; This is not a request but a promise of our responsibility as humans and as Christians. This is a calling and sacred oath for us to shape our lives around.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p><em><strong>Thy kingdom come,</strong></em></p><p>The LORD&#8217;s kingdom is coming in the eschatological (end times) sense-yes.&nbsp; But also, the kingdom is always coming and has already come in the person of Jesus.&nbsp; &#8220;The Kingdom of God is within you&#8221; &#8220;the kingdom of heaven is at hand&#8221; etc.&nbsp; Once again, this is less of a request for God&#8217;s action but an acknowledgment of it!&nbsp;</p><p><em><strong>Thy will be done, in earth as it is in heaven.</strong></em></p><p>And so this continues as we promise to make Earth like heaven.&nbsp; This is not a boast of our power, nor fantastical Utopianism it is the life of the Christian.&nbsp; We are the bring the glory of the Lord into the world as the glory of the Lord has been brought into us!&nbsp; This acknowledges both God&#8217;s supreme power that nothing can happen unless he allows it, but also our responsibility to carry out his will.&nbsp;</p><p><em><strong>Give us this day our daily bread;</strong></em></p><p>Here we acknowledge our own powerlessness, we cannot continue in our service without that spiritual food and drink of the Lord which sustains us.&nbsp; We are in need of sustaining not once, not occasionally, but always, every day.&nbsp; And so we return to Christ every day for our sustaining.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p>The point of Holy Communion and the Christian life in general is that <em>&#8220;</em>that our sinful bodies may be made clean by his body,<br>and our souls washed through his most precious blood,<br>and that we may evermore dwell in him, and he in us&#8221;  We are to become little Christs by consuming Christ!!! </p><p><em><strong>And forgive us our trespasses, As we forgive them that trespass against us;</strong></em></p><p>And the reason we need this sustaining is because we constantly destroy ourselves through our sinning.&nbsp; We ask for mercy with the promise that we will also be merciful to those who sin against us.&nbsp; We promise to love and forgive not to earn our salvation but because of it.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p><em><strong>And lead us not into temptation, But deliver us from evil.</strong></em></p><p>The prayer ends with the only actual request that we really make for the future.&nbsp; This request is not for prosperity, nor for happiness, but to give us a burden we can bear.&nbsp; This acknowledges that nothing happens without God&#8217;s willing, God only allows as many demons around a man as he can withstand.&nbsp; And so we pray for direction, and in our kneeling, looking up, and praising God we find our compass and the so prayer works.&nbsp;&nbsp;</p><p><em><strong>For thine is the kingdom, the power, and the glory, For ever and ever. Amen.</strong></em></p><p>This addendum is not in the Bible but is a liturgical invention of the first century.&nbsp; Roman Catholics separate this from the Lord&#8217;s Prayer by a few moments but still say it.&nbsp; Either way it is an embellishment.&nbsp; A beautiful, spiritual decoration.&nbsp; The wording of seems to spiral up into heaven as we lift our hearts and minds eye spiritually to
